About

I go looking for hard, interesting problems and stay until they make sense.

I'm Yucheng (Cecilia) Zhang, an M.S. student in Computer Science at Northwestern University. Before that, I earned my B.S. in Mathematics with a Computer Science minor from The University of Texas at Austin.

The world is full of messy data, scattered information, and more choices than anyone can weigh. Studying math gave me something I'm still grateful for: the ability to sit quietly with a problem and think it through on my own. I'm fascinated by the logic hiding behind things, and I want to use applied methods to analyze it, explain it, and maybe create something with real meaning and value. Honestly, that process is where I find a lot of my joy.

So far, that curiosity has taken me from credit risk to brain signals to AI tutors for programmers, and turned into production software (CodeAssist), statistical models (Toyota credit risk, RNA-seq analysis), and five peer-reviewed publications.

Current focus: LLM systems, applied ML, and model evaluation for real users.

Technical base: Python, SQL, React, Flask, PostgreSQL, Docker, Scikit-learn, TensorFlow, PyTorch.

Also: photography and painting, a slower, quieter version of the same habit of close observation.
